Loch Ness Monster spotted from space? Possible Nessie sighting made in Google Earth and Google Maps satellite images

By Philip Murray
- philip.murray@hnmedia.co.uk
Published: 14:24, 18 September 2026
 | Updated: 15:30, 18 September 2026

Has Nessie been spotted from space?

One eagle-eyed future tourist looking to research a visit to Inverness certainly thinks so.

For, in what has already been a busy year for potential Nessie sightings, the latest possible snapshot has been made on Google Earth.

And not only is there one mysterious object in the water, but three - a “little family of Nessies”, said Julie Fraser after making the discovery.

She has now reported the images to The Official Loch Ness Sightings Register and is waiting back to hear back to find out if they will be recorded as the 17th potential sighting of the legendary beast made this year.

At the time of writing, 14 in-person encounters have been officially recorded in 2026, with two further sightings made digitally through webcams of the iconic loch.

If accepted, Julie’s sighting will be the third one made digitally since January.

Speaking to The Inverness Courier, Julie, who is from the north-east of England, said: “Last weekend I opened Google Earth to look around the Clan Fraser cemetery on the east shore of the loch - I'm planning a visit to Inverness later in the year and was just playing when I spotted the three objects.

“Taking a closer look and enlarging the images they really do look like a pod of whales. Obviously there's no specific date but there's a Google 2026 and the trees are in full leaf so it's recent.

“There's an image of a pleasure boat with a wake but no wake from the three things. They don't like like boats and look like whales or something similar with an arc to their back.

“I'm not a crank. I'm not as familiar as probably local people but it definitely looks like something significant to me.”

She continued: The Loch Ness Sightings got in touch yesterday and said they're going to have a look.

“I had a look yesterday and they're still there. The thought of a little family of Nessies is so exciting.”
