 Further sightings of Loch Ness Monster brings number up to 16 for 2026 with people from Germany, Barcelona and Malta registering pictures
 By Mike Merritt 
 Published: 13:32, 13 September 2026
 | Updated: 13:44, 13 September 2026
 Felix Tiekemeier's sighting. The 16th sighting of the Loch Ness Monster this year has been logged by a foreign tourist who saw it from less than 20 feet away and it “appeared to have a head”.
 Felix Tiekemeier, visiting from Germany is the latest person to spot something mysterious in the legendary loch.
 He reported that at 11.58am on August 21 he saw a creature that was approximately six metres (19.6 feet) long at a distance of approximately 300 meters (984 feet).
 “He said that the object was black and surfaced, remained at the water's surface for about 20 seconds, and then disappeared again - he explained that it was swimming close to the surface and appeared to have a head,” said the Official Loch Ness Monster Sightings Register , which has accepted the record.
 Last month, Jocelyn Boyce, visiting from Barcelona spotted a black shape near Urquhart Castle – a favourite sighting spot for Nessie.
 It was on August 6 at 1pm she said she saw the mysterious shape.
 "I was at a viewpoint at Urquhart Castle overlooking Loch Ness with my family when we noticed a dark, almost black, mound-like shape protruding above the surface of the water,” she said.
 “I initially observed the object with my naked eye. I estimate that it was approximately half a mile away from our position.
 “The water appeared calm and general visibility was good.
 “I took approximately 13 seconds of video footage using an iPhone 16. Due to the distance, I used the phone’s zoom while recording the video.
 “The dark mound-like shape is visible above the surface of the water in one of my photographs. In another photograph taken moments later, the object is no longer visible above the water.”
 The day earlier Norbert Kawka , visiting from Malta, reported that at around 8.50pm, near Fort Augustus for 15 minutes he saw a creature move against the currrent, in an out of the water like “a very large eel”.
 The weather was foggy with a hint of rain, there was no wind and water conditions were calm.
 He estimated that the size was at least seven meters (22.9 feet) in length. There was no boat activity it left a lot of disturbance on the water surface.
 The creatures behind repeated sightings of the fabled monster may be giant eels, according to scientists in 2019.
 Seven years ago researchers from New Zealand tried to catalogue all living species in the loch by extracting DNA from water samples.
 Following analysis, the scientists ruled out the presence of large animals said to be behind reports of a monster.
 No evidence of a prehistoric marine reptile called a plesiosaur or a large fish such as a sturgeon were found.
 Catfish and suggestions that a wandering Greenland shark were behind the sightings were also discounted.
 European eels are among the creatures in the loch, and whose DNA was picked up by the research.
 Jocelyn Boyce's sighting. Juvenile eels, known as elvers, arrive in Scottish rivers and lochs after migrating more than 3100 miles (5000 km) from the Sargasso Sea near the Bahamas, where the animals spawn and lay eggs.
 Young eels arrive in the River Ness and Loch Ness every year as part of their life cycle
 Prof Neil Gemmell, a geneticist from New Zealand's University of Otago. said: "There is a very significant amount of eel DNA. Eels are very plentiful in Loch Ness, with eel DNA found at pretty much every location sampled - there are a lot of them. So - are they giant eels?
 "Well, our data doesn't reveal their size, but the sheer quantity of the material says that we can't discount the possibility that there may be giant eels in Loch Ness.
 “Therefore we can't discount the possibility that what people see and believe is the Loch Ness Monster might be a giant eel."
 The Loch Ness Monster is said to be worth £41m in tourism revenue to the region.
 Norbert Kawka's picture. Irish missionary St Columba is first said to have encountered a beast in the River Ness in 565AD.
 The official register has now logged 1180 sightings at the loch from records and other evidence stretching back through the centuries – plus 17 webcam images.
 Last year there were six accepted sightings at the loch plus four webcam records.
