_**The Bermuda Triangle**_ is a best-selling 1974 book by [Charles Berlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Berlitz "Charles Berlitz") which popularized the belief of the [Bermuda Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Triangle "Bermuda Triangle") as an area of ocean prone to disappearing ships and airplanes. The book sold nearly 20 million copies in 30 languages. [[ 1 ]]()
In the book, Berlitz elaborates upon several theories for the purported disappearances. One of those theories states that the Bermuda Triangle was actually a by-product of the destruction of [Atlantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantis "Atlantis") .
The book was the subject of criticism in [Larry Kusche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Kusche "Larry Kusche") 's 1975 work _The Bermuda Triangle Mystery—Solved_ , in which Kusche cites errors in the reports of missing ships, and has also said "If Berlitz were to report that a boat were red, the chance of it being some other color is almost a certainty." [[ 2 ]]() [Lloyd's of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd's_of_London "Lloyd's of London") has determined the Triangle to be no
more dangerous than any other piece of the ocean, and does not charge unusual rates of insurance for passage through the area. [United States Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard "United States Coast Guard") records confirm this determination. However, tales of missing ships, although promoted by Berlitz, existed prior to the book's publication.
