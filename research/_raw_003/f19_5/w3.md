The **Bermuda Triangle** , also known as the **Devil's Triangle** , is a loosely defined region in the [North Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean "Atlantic Ocean") , roughly bounded by [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida "Florida") , [Bermuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda "Bermuda") , and [Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico") .
Since the mid-20th century, it has been the focus of an [urban legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_legend "Urban legend") suggesting that many [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft "Aircraft") , [ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship "Ship") , and people have disappeared there under mysterious circumstances. However, extensive investigations by reputable sources, including the [U.S.
federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States "Federal government of the United States") and scientific organizations, have found no evidence of unusual activity, attributing reported incidents to natural phenomena, human error, and misinterpretation. # Criticism of the concept
## Larry Kusche
[Larry Kusche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Kusche "Larry Kusche") , author of _The Bermuda Triangle Mystery: Solved_ (1975), argued that many claims of Gaddis and subsequent writers were exaggerated, dubious or unverifiable. [[ 1 ]](,_1975-1) Kusche's research revealed a number of inaccuracies and inconsistencies between Berlitz's accounts and statements from eyewitnesses, participants, and others involved in the initial incidents.

...

* The numbers themselves had been exaggerated by sloppy research. A boat's disappearance, for example, would be reported, but its eventual (if belated) return to port may not have been.
* Some alleged disappearances were, in reality, not mysterious.

## Further responses
When the British [Channel 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_4 "Channel 4") television program _The Bermuda Triangle_ (1992) [[ 22 ]]() was being produced by John Simmons of Geofilms for the _Equinox_ series, the marine insurance market [Lloyd's of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd's_of_London "Lloyd's of London") was asked if an unusually large number of ships had sunk in the Bermuda Triangle area.
Lloyd's determined that large numbers of ships had not sunk there. [[ 3 ]]() Lloyd's does not charge higher rates for passing through this area. [United States Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard "United States Coast Guard") records confirm their conclusion. In fact, the number of supposed disappearances is relatively insignificant considering the number of ships and aircraft that pass through on a regular basis. [[ 1 ]](,_1975-1)

# Hypothetical explanation attempts
## Natural explanations
### Compass variations
[Compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass "Compass") issues are frequently cited in accounts of Bermuda Triangle incidents. While some have theorized that unusual local magnetic anomalies may exist in the area, [[ 35 ]]() such anomalies have not been found.
Compasses have natural [magnetic variations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_variation "Magnetic variation") in relation to the [magnetic poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_of_astronomical_bodies "Poles of astronomical bodies") , a fact navigators have known for centuries.

...

### Human error
One of the most cited explanations in official inquiries as to the loss of any aircraft or vessel is [human error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_error "Human error") . [[ 40 ]]() Human stubbornness may have caused businessman [Harvey Conover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Conover "Harry Conover") to lose his sailing yacht, _Revonoc_ , as he sailed into the teeth of a storm south of Florida on 1 January 1958. [[ 41 ]]()

### Violent weather
[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Atlantic_hurricane_tracks.jpg) Tracks of all known Atlantic hurricanes between 1851 and 2019. Many storms pass through the Bermuda Triangle.
Hurricanes are powerful storms which form in tropical waters and have cost thousands of lives and caused billions of dollars in damage. The sinking of [Francisco de Bobadilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_Bobadilla "Francisco de Bobadilla") 's Spanish fleet in 1502 was the first recorded instance of a destructive hurricane. [[ 42 ]](,_1974,_pp._25%E2%80%9328-45) These storms have caused a number of incidents related to the Triangle.
Many Atlantic hurricanes pass through the Triangle as they recurve off the [Eastern Seaboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States "East Coast of the United States") , and, before the advent of [weather satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_satellite "Weather satellite") , ships often had little to no warning of a hurricane's approach. [[ 43 ]]()

...

### Methane hydrates
Publications by the [USGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USGS "USGS") describe large stores of undersea hydrates worldwide, including the [Blake Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Ridge "Blake Ridge") area, off the coast of the southeastern United States. [[ 50 ]]() However, according to the USGS, no large releases of gas hydrates are believed to have occurred in the Bermuda Triangle for the past 15,000 years. [[ 3 ]]()
