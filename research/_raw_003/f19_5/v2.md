**Vincent Hayes Gaddis** (December 28, 1913 – February 26, 1997) was an American author who invented the phrase " [Bermuda Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Triangle "Bermuda Triangle") ", which he used first in the cover article for the 1964 February issue of the magazine [_Argosy_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argosy_(magazine) "Argosy (magazine)") .
[[ 1 ]]() [[ 2 ]]() He popularized many stories about anomalous and [paranormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranormal "Paranormal") phenomena in a style similar to that of [Charles Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Fort "Charles Fort") . [[ 3 ]]()

# Career
Gaddis was born in [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio "Ohio") to Tilden H. and Alice M. (Smith) Gaddis. He married Margaret Paine Rea on July 14, 1947. Gaddis worked as a newspaper reporter and writer-editor for a [Warsaw, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw,_Indiana "Warsaw, Indiana") , radio station from 1947 to 1952.

# Reception
Gaddis' statements on the [Bermuda Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Triangle "Bermuda Triangle") and [spontaneous human combustion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_human_combustion "Spontaneous human combustion") have been criticized by skeptics for being inaccurate and misleading.
